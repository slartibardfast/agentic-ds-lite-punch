## Method

Per cell: precondition check (tuple stable, relay pid constant), probe
series at 1 Hz from dslp-probe toward the published mapping, `kill -STOP`
the relay for the cell duration, observe, `kill -CONT`, watch the tuple for
republish, then a 60 s resurrection watch on the old tuple and a probe
switch. Liveness markers come from the eth1 capture: arrivals of the
vdsl4-masqueraded probe flow (src 84.203.115.61 to dst 192.168.0.21:40000),
converted to monotonic milliseconds via boot time. The forward leg was not
assertable in this campaign (pre-socket inbound-NEW consumption, see the
Wiring findings and RCA section above); the arrival stream is the AFTR-side
liveness truth and is what this campaign measures.

## Findings

1. Port reuse, eighteen of eighteen. Across every deliberate mapping death
   (5 to 30 s keepalive pauses) and recreation, the AFTR re-issued the
   identical external tuple 37.228.213.83:59230. Combined with the earlier
   observations (restarts, retargets), the re-issuance is the rule on this
   line, and tuple publication stays stable across churn.
2. Preconditions held in every cell (tuple stable, relay pid constant before
   the pause): the driver's abort logic never fired.
3. The arrival-based liveness sensing worked exactly as designed: every cell
   carries a measured arrival stream, the pause created the expected
   delivery gap, and the stream resumed on the re-created mapping. The rig's
   core purpose (capture-derived mapping-liveness timing under pause) is
   validated.
4. Death and recovery timing: the exact per-cell death-to-recovery gap needs
   the full per-cell arrival series (available in the raw pcaps); the
   summary columns are limited by the port-reuse wrinkle (the driver detects
   "republish" by a tuple change, which never occurred, so the post-republish
   fallback column is the cell's first arrival and is not a recovery marker).

## Limitations and carried items
