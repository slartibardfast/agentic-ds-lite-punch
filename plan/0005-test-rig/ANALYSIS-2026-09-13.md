# ANALYSIS-2026-09-13: soak timing, the AFTR refreshes its mappings on inbound

Source data: the per-cell eth1 pcaps and sink receipts of runs 1 and 2
(script: `analyze_latency.py`; raw data on the router). This annex
supersedes the death-and-recovery reading in the run result summaries.

## The central finding

The VM AFTR refreshes a UDP CGNAT mapping on inbound datagrams. RFC 7857
§7 says an inbound datagram that matches an existing endpoint-independent
mapping SHOULD NOT refresh it; this implementation does. The evidence:

- In all 36 cells across both runs the arrival stream survived the whole
  pause (`survived = y` everywhere), through 30-second keepalive silences
  on a mapping whose documented timeout is 5 to 10 s.
- The measured death delay equals the pause duration exactly (5 s cells:
  4.8 s, 10 s: 9.8 s, 12 s: 11.8 s, 20 s: 19.9 s, 30 s: 29.9 s): the last
  delivered arrival sits at the resume boundary, not at a timeout.
- The delivery gap is one probe cadence (about 1 s) in run 2 and the
  stream phase at resume in run 1: no gap forms because no death occurs.

Consequence: the A3 pause premise as executed was confounded. Stopping
only the relay keepalives does not expire the mapping while the probe's
own stream is inbound, because the probe stream itself maintains the
mapping. The 36 cells measured mapping survival under inbound traffic,
which holds trivially, not mapping death.

## What the data does measure

- Forward one-way transit, probe to sink (vdsl4 masquerade, the internet,
  the AFTR, the relay, the sink): median 14 to 19 ms in every run-2 cell,
  with the same transit in run 1 where the forward leg was reachable
  before the accept_local fix. A stable, healthy path latency.
- The single-datagram max deltas (9.9 s and 29.9 s, the echo of the pause
  durations) are the pause-era datagrams that the SIGSTOPped relay
  buffered at its socket and flushed on resume. They are flushes, not
  transit.
- Port reuse: with no death in the pause, most reuse observations are
  trivial (a mapping that never expired has nothing to re-issue). The
  genuine death plus immediate re-issue evidence remains the relay restart
  events (retarget and revert): there the mapping did die and the AFTR
  re-issued 37.228.213.83:59230, now observed across several restarts.
- Arrival continuity at the 1 Hz cadence holds in every cell of run 2,
  which also validates the leak fix end to end.

## Methodology correction

The mapping-death measurement needs a probe-quiet pause: silence both the
keepalives and the probe for the pause window, then resume both. With no
inbound traffic at all, the mapping genuinely expires at the AFTR's idle
timeout, the relay's churn detection fires on the new tuple, and the true
death-to-recovery latency becomes measurable. That is a small driver
change (stop the probe inside the pause) and the natural design of a
run 3. The AFTR's true UDP idle timeout remains unmeasured by these runs.
